# Journal and guideline findings

## Computational Materials Science

Official journal page: https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/computational-materials-science/0927-0256

The journal scope covers computational materials science, including metals, nanostructures, two-dimensional materials, and computational-method development. It states that papers focused on simulations must contain new conceptual or computational advances and that methodological/data-driven studies should provide FAIR-compatible data and code. It accepts articles, letters, perspectives, and reviews. This is the strongest current scope match for the benchmark only if the manuscript clearly demonstrates a meaningful computational or reproducibility contribution rather than presenting ordinary calculations.

## The Journal of Physical Chemistry A

Official author-guidelines page: https://researcher-resources.acs.org/publish/author_guidelines?coden=jp4caa

The ACS guidelines require complete standard research sections, numbered and labelled components, embedded figures/tables/equations at their point of relevance, complete references including titles, and Supporting Information as separate file(s). The guidelines also emphasise computational reproducibility and sufficient technical detail for readers to reproduce reported calculations. The present manuscript meets several file-level requirements but its scientific scope is better suited to a methodology/benchmark venue than to a conventional discovery article until production chemistry is completed.

## Compute/HPC evidence

GPAW documentation: https://gpaw.readthedocs.io/

Google Cloud HPC overview: https://cloud.google.com/solutions/hpc

Archived UVA chemistry/HPC guide: https://archive.rc.virginia.edu/userinfo/hpc/software/chemistry/

These sources confirm that periodic DFT can be run on MPI/Slurm or scalable cloud HPC systems, but access requires an external account, allocation, credentials, or institutionally provided cluster. No such compute endpoint is configured in the current session.

## Editorial conclusion

No journal can guarantee acceptance. The current benchmark manuscript can be prepared for a computational-methodology or reproducibility venue, but a high-impact catalyst-discovery submission still requires accepted production DFT results and corresponding source data before discovery claims can be made.
